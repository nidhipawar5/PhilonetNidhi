The Attention Economy Was Always the Wrong Frame
Margins Newsletter · March 2026

The fundamental problem isn't that we're addicted to our phones. It's that we've built platforms that reward the performance of thinking over the act of thinking itself. When engagement metrics collapse signal and noise into a single number, the rational response is to optimise for the number.

But framing this as a problem of attention misses something deeper. Attention is not the scarce resource. Genuine cognitive engagement — the kind that changes how you see something — has always been rare. We have simply built better instruments for measuring its absence.

The philosophers who worried about the printing press worried about the same thing: that the proliferation of text would produce readers who consumed without reflecting. Every technology of distribution creates a new version of the same anxiety. What's different now is the feedback loop is milliseconds, not decades.

What we need is not less content but a different relationship between reading and thinking. The moment of insight is private, but the proof of insight is social — and that inversion is what every social product has gotten wrong.
